# Bottom Bracket Standards

## Overview

The bottom bracket (BB) is the bearing assembly that connects the crankset to the frame. Dozens of incompatible standards exist — choosing correctly prevents creaking and ensures correct chainline.

## Standards Table

| Standard | Shell Width | Thread / Fit | Shell ID | Common Use |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| BSA / English | 68 mm (road), 73 mm (MTB) | 1.370"×24 tpi | 34.8 mm | Most road, MTB |
| Italian | 70 mm | M36×24 tpi | 35 mm | Older Italian road frames |
| PF30 | 68 mm (road), 73 mm (MTB) | Press-fit | 46 mm | Carbon road frames |
| BB86 / BB92 | 86.5 mm (road), 92 mm (MTB) | Press-fit | 41 mm | Shimano road/MTB |
| BB386EVO | 86.5 mm | Press-fit | 46 mm | Gravel, cyclocross |
| T47 | 68 mm / 79 mm | M47×1 mm | 46 mm | Modern threaded 30 mm |
| BB30 | 68 mm (road), 73 mm (MTB) | Press-fit | 42 mm | Older Cannondale, FSA |
| OSBB | 61 mm / 83 mm | Press-fit | 46 mm | Specialized road/MTB |

## Chainline Values

| Drivetrain | Target Chainline |
|---|---|
| Road (2×) | 43.5 mm |
| Road (1×) | 45 mm |
| MTB (3×) | 47.5 mm |
| MTB (2×) | 47.5 mm |
| MTB (1×) | 49 mm (Boost: 52 mm) |

## Compatibility Notes

- BSA and T47 are the most serviceable — threaded shells resist creaking better than press-fit.
- PF30 and BB30 use the same 46 mm bore but different shell widths — not interchangeable.
- BB86 spindles (24 mm) differ from PF30/BB30 spindles (30 mm) — check crankset spindle diameter.
- Boost (148 mm rear spacing) requires +3 mm chainline offset on MTB 1× setups.
